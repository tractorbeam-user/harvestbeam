Opening it the first time

Harvestbeam isn't signed with an Apple Developer ID yet, so macOS asks you to allow it once.

  1. Open Harvestbeam. When macOS says it can't verify it, click Done.
  2. Go to System Settings → Privacy & Security and scroll down.
  3. Click Open Anyway next to Harvestbeam and confirm with your password.

That's it. Updates install and open on their own after this.

Beta features 1.4 beta

Automatic tracking, in the spirit of Toggl: Harvestbeam notices what you're working on and fills in the gaps. Each feature is off until you switch it on in Preferences → Tracking, and what it sees stays on your Mac.

Download the beta Installs as its own app, Harvestbeam Beta, with the same sign-in. Release notes →

  • Timeline

    Notes each app and window you spend more than 10 seconds in. The Week Calendar shows your day by the hour, with the stretches no timer covered ready to log in a click.

  • Autotracker rules

    “When Figma's window says Pinstripes, it's Creative Support.” After a few seconds in a matching window, Harvestbeam offers to start that timer, or starts it for you.

  • Suggestions from your screen

    Press ⌥H and the first suggestion is the task the window you were just in points to, from your rules and where you've tracked before.

  • Meetings log themselves

    Within five minutes of a meeting starting, it's logged to the project it matches, with Undo in the notification. Meetings with no clear match wait for you.

  • Continue or stop, anywhere

    One shortcut you choose: it stops the running timer, or picks your last entry back up, from any app.

  • Private by design. Window titles and site names never leave your Mac, and only the entries you log go to Harvest. Recording skips idle time, password managers and Messages, and keeps two weeks.

    Its own app. Harvestbeam Beta sits next to Harvestbeam and updates to newer betas. Quit Harvestbeam while you use it, so only one is in the menu bar. To leave, move Harvestbeam Beta to the Trash.
